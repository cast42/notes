# NBER announces *The Economics of Transformative AI*

## TL;DR

The National Bureau of Economic Research announced a new book, *The Economics
of Transformative AI*, edited by Ajay Agrawal, Erik Brynjolfsson, and Anton
Korinek. Published by the University of Chicago Press in September 2026, the
volume brings together sixteen research studies with expert comments.

Its central contribution is a research map for a possible post-transformative-AI
economy. AI is not treated as one productivity input or one labour-market
shock, but as a technology that may reorganise how innovation, markets, firms,
knowledge, work, public finance, and human purpose fit together.

## The central idea

The book shifts the unit of analysis from **AI adoption by individual firms**
to **the redesign of the economic system around machine intelligence**. Once
machines can perform a large share of cognitive work—or act as autonomous
economic agents—the important questions become institutional:

- Where should decision rights sit when AI can codify local knowledge and
  process information at scale?
- How do markets and firms change when AI agents can transact, negotiate, and
  coordinate directly?
- How should productivity, innovation, welfare, and human contribution be
  measured when many valuable outputs are not captured by current statistics?
- Who owns the gains when AI raises output but displaces labour or concentrates
  economic and political power?
- Which policies preserve human flourishing, information quality, and agency
  under conditions of rapid capability growth?

This makes the volume less a forecast than an **economic operating map for
transformative AI**: it identifies the mechanisms and institutional choices
that determine whether greater machine capability becomes broadly shared
prosperity or increased concentration and dependency.

## What the volume covers

### Foundations

The opening studies examine the value of “genius on demand,” AI in research and
development, and the measurement problem created when AI changes the nature of
production and innovation.

### Markets, competition, and organisation

The middle section asks how competition and welfare change, what an economy of
AI agents might look like, whether AI creates a “Coasean singularity” in market
design, and how firms adapt when intelligence becomes more widely available.

### Labour, distribution, and human welfare

The labour section considers worker adaptability, work and growth in an AGI
world, fears about a post-AGI future, and the use of algorithms to improve
behavioural-economic decision-making.

### Knowledge, information, and systemic risk

The volume revisits Hayek's dispersed-knowledge problem, AI-accelerated science,
the information ecosystem, public finance, and the amount society should spend
to reduce existential AI risk.

## What is especially useful

- It connects technical capability to economic institutions instead of leaving
  “AI impact” as a vague productivity claim.
- It treats distribution and power as first-class economic variables alongside
  output and efficiency.
- It brings measurement, market design, organisational change, information
  quality, public finance, and existential risk into one research agenda.
- The chapter-and-comment format exposes disagreement and open questions rather
  than presenting one unified prediction as settled fact.

## Limits and reading stance

This is a research volume and conference report, not a single-author theory or
a validated forecast of AGI. The chapters examine different mechanisms and
scenarios; their conclusions should not automatically be combined into one
probability estimate. The durable value is the decomposition of the problem:
transformative AI changes the distribution of knowledge, decision rights,
productive power, and economic rewards, so each layer needs its own evidence
and policy response.
