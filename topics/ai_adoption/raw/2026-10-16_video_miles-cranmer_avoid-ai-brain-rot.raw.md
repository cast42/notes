## Main argument

Miles Cranmer argues that AI can improve the quality of people's work while weakening their ability to produce and judge that work themselves. Since everyone has access to similar tools, routine AI use can compress performance toward a common average: weaker performers improve most, while stronger performers gain less and may produce work that looks increasingly similar. He argues that the advantage is not merely using AI, but developing judgment to direct and evaluate it.

The summary reports that the talk contrasts using AI to obtain solutions with using it for hints or explanations. Cranmer recommends treating AI as a Socratic tutor: ask one question at a time, use hints only when needed, and make the learner explain the answer. He says he asks whether he could explain and defend an AI-generated result without the model. If not, he first learns enough to check it.
