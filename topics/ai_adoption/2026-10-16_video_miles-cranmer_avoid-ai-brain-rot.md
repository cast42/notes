# How to Avoid AI Brain Rot — Miles Cranmer

## TL;DR

- Miles Cranmer's central warning is that AI can improve the output someone produces while weakening their ability to produce and judge that work independently if they use it as a substitute for effort.
- Use AI to extend your reach, explain ideas, offer hints, test hypotheses, and handle routine work—but keep practising the skills needed to understand, direct, and evaluate the result.
- A useful personal check: could I explain and defend this result without the model? If not, learn enough to assess it before relying on it.

## Key takeaways

- **Convenience can hide a learning cost.** The talk contrasts getting an answer from AI with asking for hints or explanations. The latter keeps the learner doing more of the cognitive work. Cranmer compares this to exercise: if the machine lifts the weight, the learner's muscles do not grow.
- **Use AI as a tutor, not an answer vending machine.** Ask it to explain, give a small hint, or pose one question at a time; then try to articulate the reasoning yourself.
- **Preserve judgment, not every task.** Delegate routine work when useful, but retain enough understanding to set the goal, recognize a bad result, and defend important decisions.
- **Tool access alone may not differentiate people.** Cranmer argues that widely available AI can raise average performance while making outputs more alike. Advantage comes from choosing worthwhile problems and developing taste and evaluation skill.
- **AI can optimize for the wrong target.** It may satisfy superficial tests or produce plausible but incorrect work. Human direction and checks remain necessary.
- **Keep learning while using the tools.** Explore hard problems with AI, but continue practising rapid learning and adaptation so capability does not become dependency.

## A practical test

Before handing off a task, decide which mode you want:

1. **Learning mode:** ask for explanations, questions, and hints; do the reasoning yourself.
2. **Delegation mode:** let AI handle routine work, then check the result against evidence and understand the parts you must own.

For consequential work, do not treat confidence or a passing superficial check as proof. Try to explain the result, inspect the evidence, and test the assumptions that matter.
