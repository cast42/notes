# Raw content

Source: https://dasmag.nl/product/the-techtonic-shift


Research capture checked 2026-09-05.

Book correction
===============
The author is Paul Verhagen, not Paul Verhoeven. The English title is “The Techtonic Shift: Power, Tech, and the End of Nations”. Das Mag lists the publication date as 3 September 2026.

Evidence status
===============
The book is newly released. The available public reactions are mostly launch material and publisher-selected endorsements. They should not be treated as a mature independent review consensus.

1. Das Mag publisher page
=========================
Source: https://dasmag.nl/product/the-techtonic-shift/

Three endorsements are presented:

- Rob de Wijk, founder of HCSS and professor of international relations at Leiden University, says Verhagen makes a compelling argument that technology will determine the future of democracy, while European countries have failed to adapt their institutions. He calls the book an important perspective on Europe’s challenge and possible solutions.
- Marietje Schaake, former member of the European Parliament and Stanford fellow, describes the book as essential for understanding how technology challenges governance and the task this creates for Europe.
- Virginia Dignum, professor of responsible AI at Umeå University, praises the case that technology is political and values are not neutral, as well as the book’s historical depth and analytical clarity. Her endorsement ends with “A must read!”.

These are favorable endorsements from relevant experts, but the publisher chose them and they are not independent reviews.

2. ICC Netherlands interview
============================
Source: https://www.iccwbo.nl/news/the-end-of-the-neoliberal-era
Published: 28 August 2026. This is an interview with the author, not a review.

The interview presents the book as an attempt to explain the breakdown of the globalized order. Verhagen’s “digital Pangea” thesis says technology is moving from a unifying market infrastructure to a dividing line between the United States, Europe, and China. The interview highlights his view that Europe’s dependence on foreign technology is a sovereignty problem and that Europe should prioritize strategic autonomy rather than attempting to build everything at once.

3. University of Amsterdam launch coverage
============================================
Source: https://ivi.uva.nl/content/news/2026/09/the-techtonic-shift.html
Published: 3 September 2026.

The institutional announcement summarizes the book as an examination of technology as geopolitical power. It emphasizes the break-up of “Digital Pangea”, the treatment of information as a security asset, technology as a sovereignty issue, and openness as a potential vulnerability. It also repeats the argument that private companies have acquired far-reaching political power while democratic institutions lag behind technological change.

This is useful confirmation of the book’s framing, but it is institutional launch coverage rather than independent criticism.

Synthesis
=========
The early reaction is uniformly positive because the public evidence is dominated by endorsements and launch coverage. The recurring praise concerns timeliness, relevance to Europe, historical depth, and clear framing of technology as a political rather than neutral force. The recurring thesis is that Big Tech and strategic technologies have accumulated state-like power while national and European institutions remain designed for an earlier, less geopolitical digital order.

The main unresolved question is whether independent reviewers will find the diagnosis empirically convincing and the proposed European responses realistic. As of this check, there are no substantial independent reviews or reader ratings to answer that question.
