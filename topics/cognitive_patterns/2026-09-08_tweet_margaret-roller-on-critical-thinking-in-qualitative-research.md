# Margaret Roller on critical thinking in qualitative research

*Margaret R. Roller — @MargaretRoller*

## Contribution to cognitive patterns

### What it adds

The compilation makes a useful distinction between a mechanical procedure and a context-sensitive inquiry. In qualitative work, the transcript is not the whole observation: the interaction, what was not said, the participant–researcher relationship, and the researcher’s own influence can change the interpretation. It also treats research quality as a graded confidence judgment tied to intended use, rather than a binary valid/invalid label.

### Fit with existing patterns

- **[Check evidence quality](check_evidence_quality.md):** strong fit. Extend the collection check to ask what the recording or instrument omits, how the observer helped produce the evidence, whose voice is weak or absent, and what contextual details limit transfer. The TQF’s Credibility, Analyzability, Transparency, and Usefulness components are a useful mnemonic for the evidence path.
- **[Examine a causal claim](examine_a_causal_claim.md):** partial fit. The warning against importing linear variable-based explanations into contextual data supports separating an observed account from a causal interpretation. It does not replace the pattern’s requirement to define an intervention, alternative, outcome, time order, and identification assumptions.

### Candidate adaptation, not a new pattern yet

The material suggests a reusable **reflexive context check**:

1. Separate what was observed from what was inferred.
2. Recover the interaction, setting, and absences that the recorded artifact hides.
3. Inspect how the investigator, instrument, or prior information shaped the evidence.
4. Check whose perspective is under-represented and whether that changes the intended use.
5. Report the process and scope well enough for another reader to judge transferability.
6. Assign confidence for the stated use instead of issuing a context-free verdict.

This is worth testing as a cross-domain refinement to evidence review, but the source alone does not establish that it improves accuracy or reduces effort. It is currently too close to qualitative-research guidance to justify a separate repository pattern; a future revision should test whether the procedure helps with interviews, user research, incident reports, and other human-generated evidence.

### Limits of the source

The compilation is a curated, author-produced selection rather than a comparative evaluation of the TQF. Its claims are valuable as methodological guidance, but they do not by themselves demonstrate that the framework outperforms alternative research-quality approaches. “Multidimensional” is also a broad prescription; applying it still requires concrete questions, an explicit scope, and a stopping rule.
