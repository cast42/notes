# Start Doing Graph Theory

## TL;DR

Mikhail Lavrov’s free, open-access textbook introduces graph theory by starting with problems and showing how to model them as graphs, then develops the definitions, techniques, and proofs needed to solve them. Its teaching principle is literal: students learn mathematics by doing it. It is designed for undergraduate courses and independent study.

## What stands out

- **Model first, formalize next.** Early examples—map coloring, scheduling games, puzzles, and efficient routes—show what information a graph preserves and what details can be discarded before introducing formal definitions.
- **Proofs are part of the subject, not an assumed prerequisite.** The author explains why proof steps work and what to watch for. An appendix reviews proof-writing and induction.
- **The reader is asked to participate.** Lavrov recommends pausing at questions before reading the answers and approaching proofs skeptically: a proof is complete only when the reader is convinced or can fill any gaps.
- **Practice accompanies the theory.** Chapters include problems, examples, and algorithms, moving from graph basics and degrees through trees, matchings, coloring, planar graphs, connectivity, and flows.

## Scope and format

The book is an introductory, proof-based treatment of graph theory, the mathematics of relationships between objects. Lavrov based it mainly on lecture notes for his undergraduate graph theory course at Kennesaw State University, then expanded and reorganized them for both classroom use and self-study. The book and its website are licensed CC BY-SA 4.0 and are freely available to read online or download.
