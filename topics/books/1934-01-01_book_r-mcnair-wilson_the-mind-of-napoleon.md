# The Mind of Napoleon

## TL;DR

R. McNair Wilson’s 1934 book, subtitled *A Study of Napoleon, Mr Roosevelt, and the Money Power*, uses Napoleon to advance a political and financial argument about Franklin D. Roosevelt’s era. It is a historically situated polemic, not an unmediated account of Napoleon’s mind or a collection of his writings.

**Archive warning:** the [originally supplied record](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.66311/page/n3/mode/1up) is labeled as Wilson’s book, but its opening scan and OCR identify *Napoleon & England, 1803–1813* by P. Coquelle. An [alternate Internet Archive scan](https://archive.org/details/the-mind-of-napoleon-a-study-of-napoleon-mr-roosevelt-and-the-money-power-1934-w) has a title page matching Wilson’s book.

## What the book is about

- **Author and edition:** R. McNair Wilson; George Routledge & Sons, London, 1934. The date in this note uses 1 January as a year-only filename convention, not a verified publication day.
- **Framing:** in the preface Wilson says he wants to place Roosevelt’s contemporary struggle against what he calls the “Masters of Money” in historical context and compares Roosevelt’s project with Napoleon’s work. This is Wilson’s interpretation, not an established account of either figure.
- **Structure:** the contents list three books—*Procession into Ruin*, *Design for Civilization*, and *The Master Builder*—followed by a conclusion. Topics include revolution, the First Consul and the president, industry, finance, and downfall.
- **Historical lens:** the preface connects the economic crisis, Christian social thought, political authority, and finance. Read its causal claims against other historical sources, not as settled fact.

## Reading cautions

The text contains sweeping religious and ethnic generalizations, including antisemitic tropes about Jews and wealth; assess its characterizations critically rather than adopting them as facts. It also has an explicit 1930s political and religious agenda.

**Scope of this note:** based on the alternate scan’s title page, preface and contents, plus a check of a passage in the body—not a complete reading or verification of the book’s historical claims. The Internet Archive OCR is imperfect.
