# Forecasting: Principles and Practice, the Pythonic Way

*Rob J Hyndman, George Athanasopoulos, Azul Garza, Cristian Challu, Max Mergenthaler Canseco, and Kin G Olivares*

## TL;DR

- A free-to-read Python edition of Forecasting: Principles and Practice, based on the third edition and using open-source Nixtlaverse libraries for practical time-series forecasting.
- Covers the full workflow from visualization and decomposition through regression, exponential smoothing, ARIMA, hierarchical forecasting, advanced methods, and practical issues.

## Highlights

- Free online textbook with executable Python-oriented examples.
- Includes both statistical forecasting foundations and judgmental, hierarchical, and advanced forecasting methods.
- Adds dedicated chapters on neural networks and foundation forecasting models; the authors assume introductory statistics and high-school algebra for most sections.

## Where to start

- [The forecaster’s toolbox](https://otexts.com/fpppy/05-toolbox.html) — benchmark methods, residual diagnostics and forecast accuracy.
- [Practical forecasting issues](https://otexts.com/fpppy/13-practical.html) — seasonality, forecast combinations, outliers and missing values.
- [Foundation forecasting models](https://otexts.com/fpppy/15-foundation-models.html) — the Python edition’s chapter on newer pretrained approaches.

The book emphasizes graphs, forecast evaluation and real-data examples. It is an applied introduction rather than an exhaustive theoretical treatment; the chapter references point to deeper material.
