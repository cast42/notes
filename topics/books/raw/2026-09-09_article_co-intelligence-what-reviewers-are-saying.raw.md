## Reviews and critical responses

### Kirkus Reviews

URL: https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/ethan-mollick/co-intelligence/
Reviewer: Kirkus Reviews
Review posted: 2 May 2024

Kirkus calls the book an accessible roadmap through AI, written with authority and without tech jargon. The review highlights the need to treat AI in a human-like way while remembering that it is a machine, the danger of hallucinations, and the importance of human oversight for ethical decisions. It also notes the possibility that AI could help junior workers perform like veterans while devaluing experienced workers, and warns that managers must keep AI as a tool rather than a boss.

### Spektrum der Wissenschaft

URL: https://www.spektrum.de/rezension/buchkritik-zu-co-intelligence/2238589
Reviewer: Christoph Pöppe
Review date: 21 October 2024

Pöppe presents the practical rules as: invite AI to the table, treat it as a person while defining its role, and remain the human in the loop. The review stresses that the user must not delegate everything to the system because fluent output can conceal mistakes. It also describes Mollick’s advice as using the tools rather than hoping the technology can be put back in the bottle.

### Journal of Academic Writing

URL: https://doi.org/10.18552/joaw.v14i1.1130
Reviewer: Christopher Eaton, University of Toronto Mississauga
Published: Summer 2024

Eaton finds the book strongest where AI intersects with pedagogy, writing, and workplace practice. He values the “human in the loop” idea, the use of AI to generate alternative ideas, and the accessibility of the examples. His central criticism is that Mollick does not examine deeply enough how AI-generated language shapes the writer’s own ideas or knowledge-building. Eaton says the book offers too little data and theoretical grounding for researchers, risks becoming a puff piece in places, and is better understood as a conversation starter and accessible introduction than as a lasting research foundation.

### Journal on Baltic Security

URL: https://journalonbalticsecurity.com/journal/JOBS/article/140/text
Reviewer: Eden Elizabeth Armstrong
Published online: 31 December 2025

Armstrong recognises the book’s clarity, accessibility, and balanced discussion of AI’s opportunities and limitations, especially its treatment of education, AI literacy, and human oversight. The review deliberately takes a cautious position against Mollick’s optimism and foregrounds safety, equity, sustainability, and risk. It finds the book’s call for experimentation harder to apply in high-stakes, risk-averse settings such as professional military education, but still calls the book timely, pragmatic, and valuable.

### Education Review / Reseñas Educativas / Resenhas Educativas

URL: https://edrev.asu.edu/index.php/ER/article/download/4257/1703/9603
Review date: mid-2025

This review says the book had already received overwhelmingly positive reactions, describing it as approachable, accessible, and useful without technical expertise. It identifies the radical move of treating generative AI as an intelligent partner, while also noting anthropomorphism and the systems’ lack of judgment. The reviewer accepts the four practical rules—invite AI, stay human in the loop, define its role, and expect better future systems—but argues that Mollick does not adequately answer how to handle AI’s contradictory ethical and social effects.
