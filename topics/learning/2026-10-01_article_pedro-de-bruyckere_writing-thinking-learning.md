# Is writing really the best way to learn to think?

## TL;DR

Pedro De Bruyckere responds to Joe Cruz’s claim that the evidence for writing as a way to learn or think is weak. De Bruyckere points to research supporting **writing to learn**, while agreeing that this does not show writing is the best way to develop thinking—or the best way to assess it. Generative AI makes that last question more urgent because a submitted essay may no longer reliably show who did the thinking.

## Key distinctions

The essay separates three claims that are often blurred together:

1. Writing can support learning.
2. Writing can support thinking.
3. A written product is a good way to assess how well someone understands or thinks.

Evidence for the first claim does not establish the third. Writing can help students learn while they select, organize, and connect ideas, and the effort of making an explanation coherent can reveal gaps in understanding. But that does not prove writing is superior to conversation, debate, or oral examination as a way to learn or demonstrate understanding.

## What the research supports

De Bruyckere cites a meta-analysis by Graham, Kiuhara, and MacKay covering 56 experimental and quasi-experimental studies of students in grades 1–12. Across science, social studies, and mathematics, writing about subject content had a positive effect on learning (effect size 0.30). This is evidence for a modest average learning benefit in those educational settings—not evidence that writing is the best way to learn general reasoning, that it beats speaking, or that an essay reliably measures a student's unaided understanding.

## Assessment in the age of generative AI

If the goal is to teach writing, students still need to write. If the goal is to use writing as a learning activity, doing the writing can be part of the learning itself. If the goal is to assess understanding, a finished essay is only one possible source of evidence—and AI assistance can make its authorship and cognitive provenance less clear.

Conversation, presentation, debate, or oral defense can reveal how a student responds and reasons in the moment. De Bruyckere does not argue that these are always better or that writing should be abandoned. A combination—writing with clearly stated AI rules, followed by questions or discussion—may reveal more than either a text or an oral test alone.

## Reading stance

The useful conclusion is not “writing is the best way to think.” It is more precise: writing can be a tool for learning and a way to make thought explicit, but the value of an assignment depends on its purpose. A text can be both a learning activity and a trace that teachers can inspect; those functions should not be confused. This complements the view that writing develops thought while adding an important qualification: the educational case for writing does not settle which assessment format is best.
