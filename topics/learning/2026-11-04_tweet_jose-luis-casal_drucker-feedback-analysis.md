# Drucker’s feedback analysis: record expectations and compare outcomes

## TL;DR

José Luis Casal introduces a thread about Peter Drucker’s *Managing Oneself*: people can be mistaken about their own strengths. Drucker’s proposed feedback analysis makes expectations explicit before an important decision or action, then compares them with what actually happens. The gap becomes material for learning, not merely a score.

## The practice

1. Before a significant decision or action, write down what you expect to happen and why.
2. Record when and how you will observe the result. Drucker suggests looking back after nine to twelve months; choose a shorter or longer interval if the outcome requires it.
3. Compare the original expectation with the actual outcome. Ask what surprised you, which assumptions were wrong, and what the experience suggests about your strengths and areas to develop.
4. Repeat across decisions before drawing strong conclusions; outcomes also depend on luck, other people, and changing circumstances.

**Example:** Before introducing a new review process, predict adoption, time saved and likely friction. At the agreed review date, compare those predictions with observed use and feedback. A poor outcome alone does not prove the decision was poor given what was knowable at the time.

Writing matters here because it preserves the expectation *before* the result is known, providing a concrete basis for comparison. This is one use of writing to support thinking, distinct from the broader claim that writing is the only way to think.
