# Writing as a thinking tool

## TL;DR

Murat Demirbas argues that writing is not merely a record of finished thought: putting ideas outside the mind lets him organize them, expose gaps and discover connections. In the age of LLMs, he recommends preserving space for one's own rough thinking before turning to polished generated prose. This is a personal account and argument, not a controlled study.

## How writing helps him think

- **Processing, not just storage:** as a student, making mind maps forced him to decide how new information related to what he already knew.
- **External memory:** notes and blog posts provide material to revisit and help him reconstruct earlier understanding. His explanation about neuronal reactivation is a personal hypothesis, not a measurement in the article.
- **A check and a generator:** writing a paper review can reveal gaps in comprehension. He describes a 2018 post comparing Paxos with Nakamoto consensus: the connection developed as he wrote and became a seed for research.
- **A computational analogy:** following Manuel Blum, he compares a finite automaton with a Turing machine equipped with an external tape. The formal distinction illustrates the power of external storage; it is an analogy, not proof that humans without writing are literally finite automata or lose a particular complexity class.

## A writing practice

1. **Make a mess:** write freely, without polishing or judging; keep a running lab notebook.
2. **Tidy up:** revisit the draft critically, cut and revise, and treat multiple drafts as normal.
3. **Prefer plain language:** use concise prose to expose rather than conceal muddled ideas.
4. **Write for yourself:** find intrinsic value in thinking, rather than relying on an audience's reaction.
5. **Schedule the work:** a regular practice can encourage attention and generate ideas even before inspiration strikes.

## Writing and AI

Demirbas warns that a model-generated memo can look like evidence of clear thinking without the requester having worked through the reasoning. His stronger suggestion that an LLM cannot make a creative leap is his view, not an established result. A useful practical experiment is to write one's own rough expectation first, then use AI to challenge, expand or edit it; compare the result with immediately requesting a finished answer. Whether that improves a particular outcome remains to be tested.
