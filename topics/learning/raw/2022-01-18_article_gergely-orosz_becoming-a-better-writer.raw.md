# Source notes

This is a paraphrase of the source, not a transcript. Orosz published the guide in January 2022 and made it freely accessible in October 2026. His announcement says that developers should contribute more accounts of their own experience in their own words, amid a perceived increase in low-quality AI-generated writing.

## Article’s purpose

The guide addresses software engineers, engineering managers, and product managers who want to communicate more clearly and progress into roles with broader influence. Its advice concerns day-to-day professional writing as well as longer documents.

## Editing process

- Draft to capture content, then edit for readability and ease of understanding; seek feedback when the work warrants it.
- Reread from the recipient’s perspective. Check whether the message has enough context and whether its structure rewards the reader’s effort.
- For longer pieces, use focused paragraphs and sections, remove distracting material, and simplify long sentences.
- Use grammar and readability tools as aids. Their recommendations can be wrong or stylistically unsuitable, so the writer remains responsible for choices.
- Use bullets, numbered steps, bolding, or visuals where they genuinely improve scanability and comprehension.
- Spend editing time in proportion to the reach and importance of the writing.

## Feedback and learning

- Ask colleagues specific questions about clarity, redundancy, confusing passages, and whether the main takeaway is evident.
- Treat feedback as material for future writing, not merely as a one-time patch.
- Read and analyze strong examples in the genres one needs to write. Save useful models and borrow their structure deliberately.
- Improve through repeated practice. Choose recurring work—such as project updates, meeting summaries, proposals, or design documents—for deliberate editing and feedback rather than trying to deeply revise every message.

## Useful synthesis

The article’s practical sequence is: draft, revise for the reader, get targeted feedback, learn from examples, and repeat. A further implication is that drafting also serves the writer: externalizing an idea exposes gaps and helps develop the thought. That thinking benefit can be lost if an AI system replaces the writer’s own expression rather than helping them revise it.
