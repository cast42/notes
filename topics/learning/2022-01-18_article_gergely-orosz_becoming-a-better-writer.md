# Becoming a Better Writer

## TL;DR

Gergely Orosz’s practical guide treats writing as an important, learnable engineering skill: draft the ideas, edit for the reader, get specific feedback, study strong examples, and practise regularly. In October 2026, Orosz made the article free and urged developers to share more of their own experience in their own words rather than publish low-quality AI-generated prose. The deeper connection is that writing can improve thinking, not just package it for an audience.

## Key takeaways

- **Writing extends technical influence.** As engineers take on broader roles, writing helps them explain tradeoffs, make decisions durable, and communicate beyond their immediate team.
- **Separate drafting from editing.** First get the ideas down; then reread from the recipient’s perspective and improve structure, context, tone, and accuracy.
- **Respect the reader’s attention.** Break long work into readable chunks, keep each paragraph focused, remove filler, shorten tangled sentences, and use lists or visuals when they make the structure easier to grasp.
- **Ask for focused feedback.** Instead of “Any thoughts?”, ask what was confusing, redundant, or unclear. Keep a record of recurring suggestions and apply them to the next piece.
- **Learn by noticing and practising.** Save well-written examples of the genres you need, analyze why they work, borrow useful structures, and make time to write and revise regularly.
- **Use tools as editors, not authorities.** Grammar and readability tools can help, but Orosz cautions that suggestions may be wrong or flatten a writer’s style; accept them selectively.

## Writing as thinking

Orosz’s article focuses mainly on professional communication, but it also cites the idea that writing helps make thoughts durable and includes a “Writing is Thinking” reference. Your framing sharpens the point: better writing can be better thinking because putting an idea into words forces the writer to order it, find missing links, and discover what is still unclear. Drafting can therefore be exploratory and rough; reader-focused editing comes after the thinking has had room to develop.

This is a useful counterweight to Orosz’s warning about low-quality AI prose. AI can assist with editing and feedback, but outsourcing the entire act of expressing an experience risks removing the process that turns experience into a considered point of view.

## A useful order for practical writing

An additional framing worth keeping: start with **why** the reader should care, then establish **who** is involved or affected, and then explain **how** it works or what to do. This is a compact way to orient the reader before the details. (This was Lode’s suggested advice; it is not attributed to Orosz’s article.)
