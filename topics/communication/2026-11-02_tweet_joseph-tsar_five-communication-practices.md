# Five practices for more effective communication

## TL;DR

Communication coach Joseph Tsar presents five habits drawn from his coaching work: resolve conflict promptly and diplomatically, make ideas simple to follow, reset between emotionally demanding conversations, regulate breathing, and reflect back what the other person said. These are practitioner recommendations, not a controlled evaluation of the five practices.

## The five practices

1. **Resolve conflict promptly.** When something is uncomfortable, address it instead of letting avoidance prolong the stress. Tsar advises being calm, clear, delicate, and firm. This is best read as a prompt to address appropriate conflicts—not as a rule to confront someone immediately in unsafe or power-imbalanced situations.
2. **Make the message simple.** Reduce the effort needed to understand an idea. Use familiar analogies, concrete examples, and direct language. If the point is hard to state simply, talk or write it through until it becomes clearer.
3. **Reset your emotional state between conversations.** Tsar calls this an “energy palate cleanser”: take a short action that helps prevent the previous interaction from setting the tone for the next one.
4. **Breathe deliberately.** When stressed or anxious, slow down and attend to breathing before an important conversation. The post recommends practising diaphragmatic breathing and entering the conversation from a calmer state.
5. **Reflect back what you heard.** Listen closely, then paraphrase the other person's meaning—for example, “What I’m hearing from you is …” This gives them a chance to correct misunderstandings and signals attention.

## Reading stance

The ideas are practical coaching heuristics. The post does not provide study citations or evidence comparing these practices, so it should not be read as proof that each works for everyone or in every setting. The most directly testable habits are simplifying a message and paraphrasing: ask the listener whether the message was clear and whether the paraphrase captured their meaning.

There is a small provenance discrepancy: the post says the advice condenses coaching at Google, Anthropic, and Oracle, while the image says the author's 500+ sessions included clients from Google, OpenAI, and Microsoft. The materials do not explain the difference; neither wording establishes that those companies endorse the advice.
